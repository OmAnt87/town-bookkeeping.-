# Ohio data status

Generated by `node scripts/oh/aos-status.mjs`. 2301 governments loaded. Ohio has 88 counties, about 250 cities, 680 villages and 1,310 townships. A township serves the parts of it outside cities, and in most cases also the villages inside it, so village residents usually pay both. Public schools are run by separate school districts. Each county's file holds the county and the governments whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** each government's unaudited annual financial report, filed with the Ohio Auditor of State through the Hinkle System, as compiled in the Auditor's [Summarized Annual Financial Reports](https://www.ohioauditor.gov/references/SummarizedAnnualFinancialReports) (one workbook per entity type, year and basis of accounting), calendar years 2023-2025. The latest year in balance is used. Most townships and villages report on the regulatory cash basis; cities and most counties on GAAP.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (counties, places and county subdivisions).

## How Ohio differs
- **Functions come from the report.** Governmental funds are reported by function: general government (legislative and executive, judicial), public safety (police and fire), public works or public services, transportation, basic utility services, health, human services, conservation and recreation or leisure time, community and economic development or community environment. Courts and community development count as overhead, like general government.
- **Capital outlay** has no function, so it is spread over each government's other spending in proportion.
- **Utility funds** count operating revenue and expenses (depreciation left out) plus nonoperating revenue, capital outlay and debt payments. City and county files cover water, sewer, electric and landfill funds; village files cover all enterprise funds combined; townships seldom run utilities.
- **Left out:** intergovernmental disbursements (the receiving government reports that spending), payments to refunded bond escrow agents, borrowing, transfers, advances, sales of capital assets, gains and losses, and special and extraordinary items. Debt service leaves out principal repaid with new borrowing not spent on capital (89 governments), most likely bond anticipation notes rolled over.
- **Municipal income tax**, levied by most cities and villages, is counted with other local taxes.
