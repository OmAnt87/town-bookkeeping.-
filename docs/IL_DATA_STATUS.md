# Illinois data status

Illinois has more local governments than any other state: 102 counties, about 1,300 cities, villages and towns, and 1,426 townships in the 85 counties that have them, plus school, park, library, fire and other special districts. Townships maintain township roads, assess property and provide general assistance, and most also cover the cities and villages inside them. Each county's file holds the county and the governments whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** each government's Annual Financial Report, from the [Illinois Comptroller's local government financial databases](https://illinoiscomptroller.gov/constituent-services/local-government/local-government-division/financial-databases/), FY 2023-2024: revenues by source and expenditures by function, by fund type, and the statement of indebtedness. Every county, municipality and township must file; the latest year in balance is used.
- **Political money:** Illinois State Board of Elections [campaign disclosure data](https://www.elections.il.gov/CampaignDisclosure/DownloadCampaignDisclosureData.aspx): contributions from businesses (allowed in Illinois), unions and PACs to county, municipal and township candidates, placed by the office each candidate filed for.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (counties, county subdivisions and places).

## How Illinois differs
- **Functions come from the report.** Illinois's Annual Financial Report classifies spending by function (general government, public safety, corrections, judiciary, transportation, social services, culture and recreation, housing, environment, utilities and debt), so services and overhead are as each government reported them.
- **Funds counted.** General, special revenue, capital projects, debt service and enterprise (utility) funds. Pension trusts and other fiduciary funds (including the money county collectors hold for every taxing district), internal service funds and component units are left out.
- **Capital outlay and depreciation.** Depreciation is left out (a non-cash expense); capital outlay is counted when paid. Capital outlay has no function, so utility-fund capital outlay counts as utilities and the rest is spread over each government's services in proportion to their spending.
- **"Other" spending.** Where spending reported as "other" (with no function) is more than 15% of the total (51 governments, including Chicago, which reports most of its pension contributions there), it is shown as not broken down and the government is not scored on services or overhead.
- **Special districts are separate.** Park, library, fire protection and sanitary districts are their own governments, so municipal reports leave out services those districts provide.
