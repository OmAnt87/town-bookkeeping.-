## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. The Census counts Illinois's townships as township governments. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions and places).

## How Illinois differs
- **Special districts are separate.** Park districts, library districts, fire protection districts, sanitary districts and the Metropolitan Water Reclamation District are their own governments with their own taxes, so city and village reports often leave out services those districts provide.
- **County collectors gather property tax** for every government, so city, village and township reports show only their own levy.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Chicago scores very low.** Its Census report puts $4.5 billion under financial administration and $3.4 billion under principal repaid, far more than any other city; both count against the score (as overhead and through fiscal health). Pension contributions filed under administration may explain part of it, but the Census files do not say, so the figures are shown as reported.
- **Political money is not scored yet.** Illinois local candidates file with the State Board of Elections, so it can be added.
