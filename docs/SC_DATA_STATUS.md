## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024: taxes, charges, aid, spending by function (operations, construction, equipment), interest, principal repaid and debt outstanding, for all funds. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used. The state Revenue and Fiscal Affairs Office's Local Government Finance Report was not used: it adds each county's cities together and leaves out utilities.
- **Political money:** SC State Ethics Commission [public contribution search](https://ethicsfiling.sc.gov/public/campaign-reports/contributions): group (non-individual) contributions to candidates for county and municipal office, placed by the office sought.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer.

## How South Carolina differs
- **Schools are separate.** School districts are their own governments, so county and municipal reports do not include schools.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in North Carolina: the receiving government reports that spending.
- **Business money is allowed.** South Carolina lets businesses give to candidates, so political money counts businesses as well as unions and PACs: $3,896,272 of the $4,133,870 counted came from businesses.
