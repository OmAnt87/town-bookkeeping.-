# Vermont data status

Generated by `node scripts/vt/status.mjs`. 146 governments loaded. Vermont's 237 towns and 10 cities are its main local governments. About 30 incorporated villages are a second layer inside their towns, and the 14 counties run little more than the courthouse and the sheriff. Public schools are run by school districts funded through the statewide Education Fund. Each county's file holds the county and its cities, towns and villages.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas, Delaware and New Hampshire. The Census counts Vermont's towns as township governments. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used. Vermont has no central collection of town financial reports: each town publishes its own annual town report.
- **Education and municipal property tax by town:** Vermont Department of Taxes, [Property Valuation and Review annual report data](https://tax.vermont.gov/pvr-annual-report) (taxes and tax rates by town, tax years 2021-2024).
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions and places).

## How Vermont differs
- **Education tax on the town bill.** Each town and city bills the statewide education property tax along with its own municipal tax and pays it over to the school district. 38 of the 130 towns and cities loaded reported the whole levy to the Census as their own property tax. For those, the education tax the Department of Taxes lists for the town is taken out, so only the municipal share is counted. Each affected town's report says so and gives the amounts. Unlike New Hampshire, where the town's share has to be estimated, Vermont publishes both figures for every town.
- **Villages inside towns.** Incorporated villages run their own services (often water, sewer, electric, police or streets) and residents pay both the village and the town. Villages do not bill education tax.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas, Delaware and New Hampshire.
