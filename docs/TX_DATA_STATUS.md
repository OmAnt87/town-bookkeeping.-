## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions and places).

## How Texas differs
- **Special districts are separate.** Municipal utility districts, hospital districts and emergency services districts have their own taxes, so some services residents pay for are not in city or county reports.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** Contributions to Texas county and city candidates were not loaded.
- **Coverage is about half.** The Census estimated, or has incomplete records for, about 700 Texas governments, mostly small cities and towns, plus about 110 counties, so they are not listed. The 15 largest cities are included; McAllen is among those left out.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (122 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
