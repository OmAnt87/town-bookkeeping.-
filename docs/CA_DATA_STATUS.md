# California data status

Generated by `node scripts/ca/status.mjs`. 538 governments loaded. California has 58 counties and about 480 cities and towns. Counties run social services, public health, jails and courts for everyone, and provide city-type services in unincorporated areas. Public schools are run by school districts, and many services come from special districts. Each county's file holds the county and the cities and towns whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used. Every California county and almost every city reported to the Census in 2022, so coverage is nearly complete.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions).

## How California differs
- **San Francisco is a consolidated city and county** and reports as one city government, covering city and county services, the airport, utilities and its public hospitals.
- **Special districts are separate.** Water, fire, park and other special districts have their own budgets, so some services residents pay for are not in city or county reports.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** Contributions to California county and city candidates were not loaded.
- **Large cities often show deficits.** Borrowing is not counted as revenue, so years with heavy capital spending (Los Angeles, San Diego, San Francisco) show spending above revenue.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (76 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
- **Estimated by the Census, not reported (left out, 1):** City of Hollister.
- **Incomplete in the Census files (only debt or a few figures, or spending under a quarter of revenue; left out, 0):** none.
- **Older year used** because the latest sampled year was estimated or incomplete: none.
- **Transparency practices** have not been checked yet, so they are not scored.
