# Kansas data status

Generated by `node scripts/ks/status.mjs`. 633 governments loaded. Kansas has 105 counties, about 625 cities and about 1,300 townships. Townships govern the rural areas outside cities, mainly maintaining township roads; cities are separate from them. Public schools are run by separate school districts. Each county's file holds the county and the governments whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. The Census counts Kansas's townships as township governments. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions).

## How Kansas differs
- **Kansas City is unified with Wyandotte County** and reports as one city government.
- **County treasurers collect property tax** for every government, so city and township reports show only their own levy.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** Contributions to Kansas county and local candidates were not loaded.
- **Coverage is about a third.** The Census estimated, or has incomplete records for, about 1,200 Kansas governments, mostly townships and small cities, plus 53 counties, so they are not listed.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (50 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
