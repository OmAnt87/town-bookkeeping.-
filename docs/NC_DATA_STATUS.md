## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** each government's Annual Financial Information Report (AFIR) to the Local Government Commission, which North Carolina collects on the Census Bureau's template, as published in the Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances individual unit files](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html), FY 2022-2024: taxes, charges, aid, spending by function (operations, construction, equipment), interest, principal repaid and debt outstanding, for all funds. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used. The Treasurer's own AFIR site could not be reached (its server sends an incomplete certificate chain).
- **Political money:** NC State Board of Elections [campaign finance transaction search](https://cf.ncsbe.gov/CFTxnLkup/): contributions from political committees and non-profits to candidates for county and municipal office.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer.

## How North Carolina differs
- **Counties fund schools.** The Census counts each county's public school system as part of the county, so school operations and construction are on county reports, shown as schools and left out of the services and overhead shares, as in Connecticut, Massachusetts and Virginia. Municipalities do not run schools.
- **Payments to other governments are left out.** The receiving government reports that spending; in FY 2024 some counties (Wake and Union among them) also report their school system's funding there, which would count it twice.
- **Corporate money is banned.** North Carolina bars corporations from contributing to candidates, so political money counts PACs, unions and other organizations: $82,383 of the $802,227 counted came from unions.
