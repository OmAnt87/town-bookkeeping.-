## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions).

## How Oklahoma differs
- **Cities run on sales tax.** Oklahoma cities cannot levy a property tax for operations, so most of their revenue is sales tax.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money** comes from the Ethics Commission's bulk contribution files (run `node scripts/ok/fetch-guardian.mjs`): cash from PACs, unions and businesses to county and municipal candidates since three years ago. Each candidate committee is placed by the jurisdiction on its registration page. Few local candidates in Oklahoma raise money from organizations, so only the counties and cities where one did are scored; Oklahoma bans corporate contributions to candidates, and tribes, individuals and party committees are not counted.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (37 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
