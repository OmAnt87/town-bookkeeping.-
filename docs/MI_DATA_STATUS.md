# Michigan data status

Generated by `node scripts/mi/f65-status.mjs`. 1846 governments loaded. Michigan has 83 counties, about 280 cities, 250 villages and 1,240 townships (general law and charter). Villages are part of the township around them, so village residents pay both; cities are separate from townships. Public schools are run by separate school districts. Each county's file holds the county and the governments whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** each unit's F-65 Annual Local Unit Fiscal Report to the Michigan Department of Treasury, from the [MI Community Financial Dashboard](https://micommunityfinancials.michigan.gov/) (its public feature service at the State's gisago-qa.mcgi.state.mi.us ArcGIS server: expenditure and revenue lines by fund, and long-term debt), fiscal years 2023-2025. The latest year in balance is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (counties, places and county subdivisions).

## How Michigan differs
- **Functions come from the report.** Spending is reported by function for the general fund, other governmental funds, enterprise funds and component units (county road commissions, downtown development and brownfield authorities). Courts, community and economic development, and redevelopment and public housing count as overhead, like general government.
- **Capital outlay** has no function, so enterprise capital counts as utilities and the rest is spread over each unit's other spending in proportion.
- **Left out:** internal service funds (the departments they serve report that spending), borrowing, transfers, sales of capital assets, depreciation and extraordinary items. Debt service is counted as reported.
- **Utilities run by separate boards** that do not report through the city's F-65, such as Lansing's Board of Water and Light, are not included; the Census counted them.
- **Tax bills:** townships and cities collect school and county taxes but report only their own levy.
