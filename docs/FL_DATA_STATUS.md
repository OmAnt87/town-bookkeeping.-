# Florida data status

Generated by `node scripts/fl/status.mjs`. 477 of the 477 governments EDR lists are loaded. Florida has no townships: counties serve everyone, and cities, towns and villages are a second layer inside them; Jacksonville is consolidated with Duval County. Public schools are run by separate school districts. Each county's file holds the county and the municipalities whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue and spending:** FL Office of Economic and Demographic Research, Expenditures and Revenues Reported by Florida's [County](https://www.edr.state.fl.us/Content/local-government/data/revenues-expenditures/cntyfiscal.cfm) and [Municipal](https://www.edr.state.fl.us/Content/local-government/data/revenues-expenditures/munifiscal.cfm) Governments: each government's Annual Financial Report to the Department of Financial Services, by Uniform Accounting System account code and fund type, FY 2008 on. Governmental and enterprise funds are counted; internal service, fiduciary (pension, trust, custodial) and component unit columns, transfers, borrowing and other financing are left out.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer.

## How Florida differs
- **Schools are separate.** School districts are their own governments, so county and municipal reports do not include schools.
- **Counties include the constitutional officers** (sheriff, clerk of court, property appraiser, tax collector and supervisor of elections).
- **Enterprise funds use accrual accounting**, so utility spending includes depreciation rather than capital purchases, and interest on enterprise debt is part of enterprise expenses.
- **Debt outstanding is not in the EDR data.** It comes from the U.S. Census Bureau's Annual Survey of Local Government Finances unit files instead (`node scripts/census-debt.mjs --state FL`), for the latest year each government reported itself: 385 of 477 governments. The rest reported no debt lines or had their figures estimated by the Census, so fiscal health is not scored for them. Census debt includes enterprise (utility, airport, port) debt.
- **Political money is not scored.** County candidates file campaign reports with the county supervisor of elections and municipal candidates with the city clerk; there is no statewide source for local candidates.

## Known gaps
- **No Annual Financial Report in the EDR data since FY 2022 (left out, 0):** none.
- **Reports since FY 2022 left out because revenue and spending are more than four times apart** (an entry error or a part left out; 0): none.
- **Not matched to Census geography:** none.
- **Older year used** because the latest year is empty or inconsistent: Town of Callahan (FY 2023; FY 2024 inconsistent), City of Cocoa Beach (FY 2023; FY 2024 inconsistent).
- **Transparency:** one of the six practices is checked from state records: whether each government has filed its annual financial report with the Department of Financial Services for the latest year most have filed. 11 of 477 have not (City of Cocoa Beach, Town of Marineland, Town of Bell, Town of Campbellton, Town of Bronson, City of Hialeah Gardens, Town of Callahan, Town of Cinco Bayou, Town of Cloud Lake, City of Port Richey, Town of Raiford). The other five practices (budget and checkbook online, competitive bidding, recorded meetings, conflict-of-interest disclosures) have no statewide source yet, so they are left out of the score.
