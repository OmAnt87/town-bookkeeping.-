# Indiana data status

Generated by `node scripts/in/status.mjs`. 1241 governments loaded. Indiana has 92 counties, about 120 cities, 450 towns and 1,008 townships. Townships cover the whole state, cities and towns included, so most residents pay a county, a township and, in a city or town, a municipality. Township trustees provide poor relief and, in many townships, fire protection. Public schools are run by separate school corporations. Each county's file holds the county and the governments whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. The Census counts Indiana's townships as township governments. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used. Indiana's Gateway portal holds each government's annual financial report, but the Census files are used so Indiana is built the same way as its neighbors.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions).

## How Indiana differs
- **Indianapolis is consolidated with Marion County** and reports as one city government, which serves the whole county except the excluded cities of Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway.
- **County hospitals are included.** Several counties own hospitals, which the Census counts as county spending (Hamilton County's is most of its budget).
- **County treasurers collect property tax** for every government, so city, town and township reports show only their own levy.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** County and local candidates file campaign finance reports with their county election board, not with the state.
- **Indianapolis is not listed:** the Census estimated most of its figures in every year.
- **Many township records are incomplete** in the Census files (no property tax, or no operating spending), so about 250 townships are left out.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (10 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
