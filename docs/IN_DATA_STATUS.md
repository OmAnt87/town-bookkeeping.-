# Indiana data status

Generated by `node scripts/in/gateway-status.mjs`. 1643 governments loaded. Indiana has 92 counties, about 120 cities, 450 towns and 1,008 townships. Townships cover the whole state, cities and towns included, so most residents pay a county, a township and, in a city or town, a municipality. Township trustees provide poor relief and, in many townships, fire protection. Indianapolis is consolidated with Marion County; the consolidated city and the county are listed separately. Public schools are run by separate school corporations. Each county's file holds the county and the governments whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** each government's Annual Financial Report, filed with the Indiana State Board of Accounts through [Indiana Gateway](https://gateway.ifionline.org/public/download.aspx) (detailed receipts, disbursements by fund and by fund and department, and debt), calendar years 2023-2024. These are cash receipts and disbursements on the state's uniform chart of accounts, reported by every government; the latest year in balance is used. (The Census unit files used for neighboring states have estimated figures for many Indiana townships and towns.)
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (counties, county subdivisions and places).

## How Indiana differs
- **Spending is assigned to services by fund and department.** Indiana's chart of accounts has no function codes. A department (police, fire, street, parks) or fund (motor vehicle highway, fire, sewage utility) names the service; debt payments are debt service and township assistance is health and human services. Other funds with general names count as general government.
- **General funds without departments.** Many towns, and some counties and cities, report their general fund without departments. Where that is more than 15% of spending (303 governments), it is shown as spending not broken down, and the government is not scored on services or overhead; a smaller remainder counts as general government.
- **Money held for others is left out.** Counties collect property and local income taxes for every government in the county and pay them over; those settlement and distribution funds, payroll and clearing accounts, trusts and escrow are left out, as are transfers between funds, purchases and sales of investments, and borrowing.
- **Refinancing is left out of debt service.** Notes and bonds paid off with new borrowing during the year (136 governments) are not counted as spending; construction paid for with borrowing is.
- **Distributions to other governments are left out**, as in the Census-based states: the government receiving them reports that spending.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** Indiana local candidates file campaign finance reports with their county election board, not with the state.
- **Not filled in (left out, 2):** PRAIRIE CREEK TOWNSHIP, WHITEWATER CIVIL TOWN.
- **Revenue and spending more than four times apart in every year (left out, 5):** ARMSTRONG TOWNSHIP, EDEN TOWNSHIP, PERRY TOWNSHIP, ROUND GROVE TOWNSHIP, STONEY CREEK TOWNSHIP.
- **Earlier year used** because the latest report was missing or out of balance: Cedar Creek Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Franklin Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Gilboa Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Greenville Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Harrison Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Jefferson Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Kirkland Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Monroe Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Perry Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Salt Creek Township (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Town of Center Point (2023; 2024 empty), Town of Kewanna (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Town of Millhousen (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Town of Mount Carmel (2023; 2024 empty), Town of Somerville (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Town of Spring Hill (2023; 2024 inconsistent), Town of Universal (2023; 2024 empty), Town of Wolcottville (2023; 2024 empty).
- **No Census geography matched (left out, 6):** ADAMS TOWNSHIP, Franklin Union Needham Township, GREENE TOWNSHIP, PINES CIVIL TOWN, Victoria Woods Civil Town, WASHINGTON TOWNSHIP.
- **County hospitals are not included.** County-owned hospitals (such as Schneck Medical Center in Jackson County) file their own reports; the Census unit files count their charges as county revenue, so those counties look much smaller here than in the Census figures.
- **Cash basis.** Receipts and disbursements are cash in and out of each fund during the year, not accrual figures; a year with a large construction project shows its spending when paid.
- **Bonds issued for other governments.** A few cities issue bonds for a school corporation or other body and pay the proceeds over as capital spending (for example Fishers' bonds for Hamilton Southeastern Schools); that spending is still counted.
- **Transparency practices** have not been checked yet, so they are not scored.
