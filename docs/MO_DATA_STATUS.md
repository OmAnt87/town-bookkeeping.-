## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. The Census counts Missouri's townships as township governments. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions and places).

## How Missouri differs
- **St. Louis is outside any county**, so its report covers both city and county services.
- **County collectors gather property tax** for every government, so city, town, village and township reports show only their own levy.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** Contributions to Missouri county and local candidates were not loaded.
- **Coverage is about half.** The Census estimated, or has incomplete records for, about 730 Missouri governments, including 57 counties and St. Joseph, so they are not listed.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (27 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
