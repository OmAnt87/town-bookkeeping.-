## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions).

## How Hawaii differs
- **Counties are the only local governments.** They provide the services cities provide elsewhere, so county overhead and services are measured like a city's.
- **Honolulu** is a consolidated city and county and is shown at the county's center.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Data checks** (`js/engine/validate.js`, run by the app, the builds and `npm test`): every record passes.
- **Political money** comes from the Campaign Spending Commission's open data (contributions to state and county candidates since 2015; run `node scripts/hi/fetch-csc.mjs`). Contributions from businesses, unions and PACs to each county's mayor, council and prosecuting attorney candidates since three years ago are counted; individuals, family, in-kind gifts and party and candidate committees are not.
- **Estimated by the Census, not reported (left out, 0):** none.
- **Incomplete in the Census files (only debt or a few figures, or spending under a quarter of revenue; left out, 0):** none.
- **Older year used** because the latest sampled year was estimated, incomplete or missing most of its revenue: none.
- **Transparency practices** have not been checked yet, so they are not scored.
