# Colorado data status

Generated by `node scripts/co/status.mjs`. 304 governments loaded. Colorado has 64 counties and about 270 cities and towns, and no townships. Counties provide city-type services in unincorporated areas. Public schools and many special districts (metropolitan, fire, water, park and recreation districts) are separate governments. Each county's file holds the county and the cities and towns whose people mostly live in it.

## Sources
- **Revenue, spending and debt:** what each government reported to the U.S. Census Bureau's [Annual Survey of State and Local Government Finances](https://www.census.gov/programs-surveys/gov-finances.html) (individual unit files), FY 2022-2024, with the same mapping as the Carolinas and Delaware. 2022 is a Census of Governments year covering every government; later years cover a sample, so each government's latest reported year is used. Almost every Colorado county, city and town reported to the Census in 2022, so coverage is nearly complete.
- **Population:** U.S. Census Bureau 2025 population estimates. **Map location:** Census 2024 Gazetteer (county subdivisions).

## How Colorado differs
- **Denver and Broomfield are consolidated cities and counties** and report as one city each, covering city and county services.
- **Special districts are separate.** Colorado has many metropolitan, fire, water and park districts with their own taxes, so some services residents pay for are not in city or county reports.
- **Payments to other governments are left out**, as in the Carolinas and Delaware.

## Known gaps
- **Political money is not scored.** Contributions to Colorado county and city candidates were not loaded.
- **Refinancing left out of debt service (17 governments):** principal repaid with new borrowing that was not spent on capital that year is treated as refinancing or a rolled-over note and left out; each affected report says how much.
